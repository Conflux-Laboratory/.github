**Conflux Laboratory** is an independent applied-research laboratory working at the confluence of computer science, quantitative economics, and humanitarian practice. We build, evaluate, and openly critique the computational methods used to allocate capital, measure risk, and deliver aid — and we study the consequences of those methods for the institutions and the people who bear them.

The name is the argument. *Conflux*, from the Latin *confluentia*, is the point where two rivers merge into a single stream. Three confluences define the work: **disciplinary** — computer science, quantitative economics, and humanitarian studies; **evidential** — market data, operational records from aid organisations, public statistics, and qualitative field evidence; and **sectoral** — capital markets and humanitarian response, two domains almost never studied side by side. We treat them as two expressions of the same methodological problem: making high-stakes decisions under uncertainty, at scale, on incomplete data.

## Research pillars

**I · Quantitative Finance & RegTech**
Applied machine learning and statistical modelling for financial markets, credit and operational risk, evaluation methodology for algorithmic trading, and the automation of regulatory workflows — including supervisory tools that stay interpretable to the humans who have to sign them off.

**II · AI for Humanitarian Action**
Computational methods for humanitarian operations: early warning for displacement and food insecurity, forecasting needs across protracted crises, operational analytics for non-governmental organisations, and responsible data practice for work with vulnerable populations.

**III · AI Governance & Societal Impact**
A cross-cutting programme: algorithmic audit, ethics and risk review of deployed systems, and policy research on the regulation of AI in finance and in humanitarian settings. It exists because the questions raised in the first two pillars — fairness, interpretability, liability, provenance — are the same questions, and benefit from being answered together.

## Current work

**Allot** — a cross-cultural study of how people allocate scarce humanitarian aid. Participants face dilemmas under genuine scarcity, where no win–win option exists; every choice is decomposed along seven ethical axes and collected as an anonymous research dataset. The design is cryptographically pre-registered before data collection, and the dataset will be published under CC-BY-4.0. It exists to inform — and to constrain — the allocation systems that come next.

**Child-labour detection** — a computer-vision method supporting a Child Labour Monitoring and Remediation System, in pilot with a partner organisation. Deployed at the edge; sensitive imagery stays on site, no personally identifying data is retained, and every finding goes to human review.

## How the laboratory works

- **Applied, not demonstrative.** Methods, evaluations, and critique that institutions can act on — not demonstrations.
- **Open by default.** Working papers and methods without paywalls; code and data released wherever it is ethically permissible.
- **Partnership over consulting.** Partners are co-investigators, and every engagement produces a public artefact.
- **Rigour.** Every claim is supported by code, data, or documented argument. No demonstrations without methods.
- **Ethics in the design, not at the gate.** We do not separate "the model" from "what the model is used for". Both are part of the research object.

## Red lines

We do not build or license systems that compute a "minimum necessary" level of survival, ration knowledge or medicine for institutional convenience, re-identify people from de-identified data, or optimise anyone's decline. This constraint travels with the work, including into downstream use.
